# Methodology

## Objective
Model the fan/customer lifecycle needed to answer **What is happening? Why does it matter? Where are the opportunities?**

## Evidence model
1. Reported benchmarks remain untouched and clearly labelled.
2. The match table is hybrid: public fixture/results, synthetic attendance/ticket allocation estimates.
3. Synthetic microdata represents 30,000 modeled fans. It is not an estimate of the full Manchester City CRM database.
4. Lifecycle and segment labels are analytical constructs, not claims about internal club taxonomy.

## Calibration
- Premier League home attendance estimates average exactly 52,517.
- 27 home-match ticket allocations sum to 1,400,000 as a season calibration constraint.
- UK fans have higher modeled matchday propensity; members have higher ticket/retail/engagement propensity.
- Digital interaction is higher for younger cohorts and members.
- Retail and membership prices are plausible modeled values, not asserted City Shop historical prices.

## Scope boundary
The modeled Ticket + Retail + Membership revenue is a sample-economy metric. It is deliberately **not reconciled to** the club's reported £340.4m commercial or £75.1m matchday revenue.
